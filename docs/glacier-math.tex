\begin{abstract}
A firm buying LLM inference by the token can instead buy GPUs, pay for power, and serve an
open-weight model itself. The per-token saving is real and large today, but it is a
\emph{melting} asset: API prices fall exponentially while electricity drifts upward, and
capturing the saving requires paying a fixed toll of integration labor and hardware. We
give the deterministic cash-flow model: closed forms for both paths, a melt time $t^\ast$
and a harvestable glacier value $G$, a two-gate decision rule that separates the marginal
condition from participation, and the force-vector decomposition of Token Yield under
rising electricity prices. Calibrated to August-2026 estimates, the participation gate
binds near $1.5$--$2\times 10^{10}$ movable tokens per month against frontier APIs, and is
essentially never cleared against hosted open-weight serving absent provider-grade
utilization or data-residency constraints. Every numerical claim is reproduced by
\texttt{dashboard/verify.mjs} in the accompanying repository.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
Time $t$ in years, horizon $T$, continuous discount rate $r$. Movable demand (the share
$\sigma$ of workload open models can serve) is $Q(t)=\sigma Q_0 e^{gt}$ reference tokens
per year. The frontier API charges $p(t)=p_0 e^{-\lambda t}$ per token
($\lambda=\ln 2/t_{1/2}$); hosted open-weight serving charges $p_o(t)=p_{o0}e^{-\lambda_o t}$
per \emph{open} token. Quality parity requires $\kappa\ge 1$ open tokens per reference
token, so the comparator price per reference token is $c(t)=p(t)$ or $\kappa p_o(t)$.

A GPU slot costs $h$ all-in, draws $w$ kW at PUE $\varphi$ \emph{whenever powered}, and
serves $s(t)=s_0e^{\mu t}$ tokens/s at utilization $u\in(0,1]$. Electricity costs
$e(t)=e_0e^{\gamma t}$ per kWh. Dev cost $D$ is paid once; MLOps runs at $m$ per year;
hardware value decays at $\delta$, salvaging at $he^{-\delta(T-t_{\mathrm{buy}})}$.

\paragraph{Electricity per token.} One GPU-hour consumes $w\varphi$ kWh and produces
$3600\,s(t)\,u$ tokens:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon(t)=\frac{w\varphi\,e(t)}{3600\,s(t)\,u}
=\varepsilon_0\,e^{(\gamma-\mu)t},\qquad
\varepsilon_0=\frac{w\varphi e_0}{3600\,s_0 u}.
\label{eq:eps}
\end{equation}
Idle power is paid, which is why $u$ sits in the denominator. At central calibration
$\varepsilon_0=\$0.172$ per million open tokens.

\section{The two paths}
\paragraph{Buy.} $\mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{buy}}
=\int_0^T c(t)Q(t)e^{-rt}\,dt
= c_0 Q_0^{\sigma}\,\frac{1-e^{-(\lambda_c+r-g)T}}{\lambda_c+r-g}$,
with $Q_0^\sigma=\sigma Q_0$ and the removable singularity
$\lambda_c+r-g\to0$ giving $c_0Q_0^\sigma T$; convergence as $T\to\infty$ iff
$\lambda_c+r>g$. The implementation uses the exact monthly geometric sum
$c_0 Q_0^{\sigma,\mathrm{mo}}(1-\rho^{M})/(1-\rho)$, $\rho=e^{(g-\lambda_c-r)/12}$,
$M=12T$ (month-start convention).

\paragraph{Build.} Fleet tranches $N_i=\max\!\big(N_{i-1},\lceil \kappa q_i/\mathrm{cap}_i\rceil\big)$
purchased when demand arrives; capacity per GPU-month $\mathrm{cap}_i = s(t_i)u\,Y/12$;
overflow above capacity is bought at $c(t)$. Then
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{build}} = D+\mathrm{PV}(\mathrm{capex})
+\int_0^T\!\Big[N(t)w\varphi\,\bar h\,e(t)+m+c(t)\,\mathrm{over}(t)\Big]e^{-rt}dt
-\mathrm{PV}(\mathrm{salvage}),
\end{equation}
with $\bar h=8766$ h/yr. $\mathrm{NPV_{adv}}=\mathrm{PV_{buy}}-\mathrm{PV_{build}}$;
build iff $\mathrm{NPV_{adv}}\ge0$. $D$ enters additively, so the maximum justifiable dev
cost is $D_{\max}=D+\mathrm{NPV_{adv}}$.

\section{The glacier equations}
The per-reference-token margin on a variable-cost basis is
\begin{equation}
a(t)=c(t)-\kappa\varepsilon(t)=c_0e^{-\lambda_c t}-\kappa\varepsilon_0e^{(\gamma-\mu)t}.
\end{equation}

\begin{prop}[Melt time]
If $c_0>\kappa\varepsilon_0$ and $\lambda_c+\gamma-\mu>0$, the margin closes at
\begin{equation}
t^\ast=\frac{\ln\!\big(c_0/(\kappa\varepsilon_0)\big)}{\lambda_c+\gamma-\mu}.
\end{equation}
If $\lambda_c+\gamma-\mu\le0$ the margin never melts; if $c_0\le\kappa\varepsilon_0$ it is
already melted (or opens later when $\lambda_c+\gamma-\mu<0$).
\end{prop}

\begin{prop}[Glacier value]
Over $\tau=\min(T,t^\ast)$, the discounted harvestable margin is
\begin{equation}
G=\sigma Q_0\!\left[c_0\frac{1-e^{-(\lambda_c+r-g)\tau}}{\lambda_c+r-g}
-\kappa\varepsilon_0\frac{1-e^{-(r+\mu-\gamma-g)\tau}}{r+\mu-\gamma-g}\right],
\end{equation}
each bracket collapsing to $\cdot\,\tau$ at its removable singularity.
\end{prop}

\begin{rule*}[Two gates]
\emph{Gate 1 (marginal): $a(0)>0$. Gate 2 (participation):}
\begin{equation}
G \;\ge\; \underbrace{D+\mathrm{PV}(\mathrm{capex})+\mathrm{PV}(m)-\mathrm{PV}(\mathrm{salvage})}_{\text{the toll}}.
\end{equation}
Gate 1 without Gate 2 is the mid-size trap: worth routing at the margin, not worth the
fixed cost. This is the cash-flow instantiation of Propositions 1--2 of \emph{Two Gates
and a Half-Life}; the block share $\sigma$ is the $\gamma_{\mathrm{sub}}\to0$ member of its
heterogeneous-substitutability family.
\end{rule*}

Note the sign of $\partial t^\ast/\partial p_0>0$ and $\partial G/\partial p_0>0$:
\textbf{frontier price cuts shrink the glacier and shorten the harvest} --- the incumbent's
routine price cuts are also its defense against self-hosting, agreeing in sign with the
melt-law feedback of the companion paper (a 30\% cut there returns $\approx4.6$pp of
workload and stretches the sector half-life from $4.0$ to $5.9$ years).

\section{Force vectors on Token Yield}
Token Yield is $y=v/\text{cost}$. Buying: $d\ln y_{\mathrm{buy}}/dt=+\lambda_c$
($\approx46\%/$yr at central calibration). Building, with electricity share
$s_E(t)$ of all-in cost:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\ln y_{\mathrm{build}}}{dt}=(\mu-\gamma)\,s_E(t)\;+\;\text{(fixed-cost dilution)}.
\end{equation}
The \emph{downward force vector} of rising electricity is $\gamma s_E \approx
0.1$--$0.3\%$/yr at $s_E=1.7$--$4.6\%$, $\gamma=6\%$/yr: real, compounding, and two orders
of magnitude smaller than the buyer's tailwind $\lambda_c$. Electricity \emph{price} nibbles
the decision; electricity \emph{capacity} can veto it: in the power-limited regime the
companion calibration gives $\partial\ln Y/\partial\ln e=-s_e\approx-0.085$ against
$\partial\ln Y/\partial\ln E_{\mathrm{delivered}}=+1/\epsilon$, a $4$--$24\times$ stronger
channel. Watch the megawatts, not the \$/kWh.

\section{Limitations}
Deterministic paths (no option value of waiting); scalar $\kappa$ for a substitutability
curve; exogenous $\lambda$ despite the defensive-pricing feedback; integer-GPU thresholds;
no latency, data-gravity, or compliance terms --- these last are reasons to build at
negative $\mathrm{NPV_{adv}}$ and belong as qualitative overrides. Calibration is
order-of-magnitude, knowledge-based as of August 2026.
